My story: three switches, one method

The cross-industry builder: factory discipline, tester scepticism, AI speed. I did not start in AI. I switched into it — three times. Each switch looked like starting over. Each one actually added a layer I still use every day.

1. Chemical engineering — learn the process before you optimise it

Engineering taught me to think in systems: inputs, processes, outputs, and safety margins. A small deviation upstream compounds downstream. The lesson I kept: do not optimise a step you have not understood, and measure what actually matters, not what is easiest to count.

2. Chip manufacturing at Intel — precision is not optional

In chip manufacturing, a tiny defect is not a rounding error. Yield, quality, and complex interdependent systems decide whether anything ships at all. The lesson I kept: quality is not a final check at the end. It is built into every step — and if you cannot measure it, you cannot trust it.

3. Automation testing at Katalon — break it before users do

In automation testing, my job was to find failure modes early. "It runs" was never the same as "it works." The lesson I kept, and the one AI made urgent: code that runs can still fail — on security, on scale, on edge cases. Evaluating output is a skill in itself, not an afterthought.

What I carry into AI products

Put those three layers together and you get how I build now: ship small, evaluate output against a clear standard, log the real mistakes, and turn recurring mistakes into rules. That is the Trace-to-Skill method behind PracticeLoop — a Skill Box built from your own errors, not from another course.

AI is changing workflows and roles fast. The defence is not knowing one tool. It is learning deliberately, every day, with reps — not just watching, but doing, checking, and fixing one thing at a time. That is what I teach, what I consult on, and what I am building in public on this site.
